Adjustment Rules
  • Follow the miss. Ball finishes left of the pocket → move feet and eyes left. Finishes right → move right.
  • 1-and-1 (parallel): feet and eyes move the same number of boards. Same angle, for small misses (1–2 boards at the pins).
  • 2-and-1: feet move 2 for every 1 with your eyes. Changes the angle as well as the position: use it when the lane changes, not for one bad shot.
  • One change at a time, then give it two good shots before judging it.
  • Not just feet: ball change, speed ±0.5 mph (faster = later hook), or less rotation in the hand (straighter).
  • Unsure? Put your line in Trajectory and tap a correction, or Find Optimal Trajectory for the best strike line in the current conditions.
Lane Status: What to Expect

Usual order on a league night: Fresh → Transition → Breakdown, with Carrydown or Burnout late. Set each game's status in the log and the Trajectory tab shows that lane.

Centers: League Night Lanes

🎳 Classic Bowl · Brunswick

Recreational THS · 40 ft · 24.5 mL · 11:1 · breakpoint target ≈ board 9

  • Lane read: heavy oil block in the middle (about boards 12–28) with a sharp "cliff" from board 8 out. Slick Brunswick lanes mean a long skid, then a sharp turn when the ball finds the dry.
  • Start with the Zen Soul: it gets cleanly through the heavy middle and reacts quickly at the cliff. Go to the Viking if the ball skids through the cliff and hits light.
  • Model start for your release:
  • Play the cliff: get the ball out to board 8–9 and let it turn. Further outside than ~7 it grabs hard and goes high, so missing right is riskier than missing left.
  • As it breaks down: boards 8–10 dry and the cliff moves inside. Move left (2-and-1) and keep the breakpoint around 9.

🪐 Planet Bowl · AMF / QubicaAMF

Standard League THS · 42 ft · 25.5 mL · 8.5:1 · breakpoint target ≈ board 11

  • Lane read: oil fades gradually out toward board 5, with no hard wall. The AMF surface grips sooner, so the ball reads earlier and arcs smoothly instead of "bouncing".
  • Start with the Viking Conquest: solid 2000-grit cover and asymmetric core, so it reads the longer pattern early and rolls strong through the pins.
  • Model start for your release:
  • Play it tighter: keep the line more direct. Don't throw it wide expecting it to come back; the gradual outside won't hold it the way Classic's cliff does.
  • As it breaks down: the blended oil carries down. Weak 10-pins or sliding past the pocket → move right 1-and-1, or switch to more surface (Storm Lock).
  • String pinsetters: pins deflect less, so good hits leave more corner pins. A strong roll and solid entry angle matter more, and so does your spare shooting.

Model lines use eyes on board 15 and your current release on the Trajectory tab. The pattern sheet's suggested lines (slide 18–20 → arrow 10 at Classic, slide 14–16 → board 12 at Planet) suit lower rev rates; at 400 rpm they go high in the model.

League Night: Game by Game

Game 1: Fresh oil · Viking Conquest

  • Start at 28 at the foul line, eyes 15. Keep speed at 16 mph and the release relaxed.
  • Pocket → stay put. Use the first 2–3 frames to learn the lane, not to chase it.
  • As the track dries, the ball starts hooking earlier and hitting high. Move left, usually 2-and-1, to stay ahead of it.

Game 2: Transition · switch to Zen Soul

  • Trigger: the Viking hooks early and dies. Weak hits (flat 10, 8-10, 5-7), or high hits even after moving left.
  • Don't force the solid by throwing it farther right or lofting it. Put it away.
  • Start the Zen Soul at 30 at the foul line, eyes 15. Throw one good shot, then follow the miss. Don't stack a ball change and a big move before seeing a shot.

Game 3: Late night · two things can happen

  • Fronts keep drying (ball still hooks early / high) → keep moving left and deeper with 2-and-1 moves.
  • Carrydown (oil pushed down the lane: ball skids long, hits light, won't come back) → move right 1-and-1, or go back to a stronger ball (Viking, or Storm Lock if it's really slick), or slow down slightly.
  • Tell them apart by where the ball goes wrong: early and high = dry; late and light = carrydown.

Spare System (TZone)

Eyes always on the center arrow (board 20). Only your mark at the foul line moves. Flat hand, no rotation, walk straight to your target.

PinFoul LineEyes
  • Multi-pin leaves: line up for the pin closest to you, e.g. 3-6-10 → shoot the 3-pin line nudged half a board toward the 6, or switch to your strike ball for big clusters.
  • Corner pins (7, 10): trust the cross-lane angle. Most misses come from drifting or turning the hand, not the line.
  • Splits: take the sure pin unless the pins are side by side.

Straight lines from the foul-line board through board 20 at the arrows to each pin.

Your release. Rotation = side spin (more hook); tilt = how far the spin axis leans (longer skid). My Profile resets them.
Surface
Ball surface compared with its factory finish. Sanded grips sooner; polished goes longer.
Breakpoint
Furthest-right board the ball reaches before turning back, and how far down the lane.
Entry angle
Angle into the pocket. About 4–6° carries best.
Roll point
Where the ball stops sliding and rolls. Too early means it runs out of energy.
Skid / Hook / Roll
Path colours: sliding through oil, turning on the dry, then rolling straight into the pins.
Strike score
0–100 rating of a line's strike chance: pocket, entry angle, miss room, roll timing and exit board.
Corrections
When your line misses the pocket: keep the foul line and move your eyes, keep the arrows and move your feet, or a 2-and-1 move. Each shows its strike score; ★ marks the best. Tap one to apply it.
